# Precise research prose

Use [Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) as an editorial prompt, not a detector or a scientific reporting standard. Its examples concern Wikipedia and do not automatically transfer to engineering papers. Style cannot establish who wrote a passage.

Revise for observable meaning:

- Replace promotional claims, inflated significance, generic praise, and vague usefulness with a specific mechanism and supported consequence.
- Name the actor, artifact, action, and relevant boundary. Resolve vague antecedents and explain specialized terms once.
- Remove repetitive summaries, unnecessary rhetorical contrasts, forced groups of three, ornamental transitions, and repeated abstract nouns when they obscure the argument.
- Use natural sentence variety without random synonyms for stable technical terms. Keep NFR, definition, taxonomy publication, dataset, run, and assessment consistent.
- Avoid vague attribution such as “studies show.” Cite the actual study and state what it examined.
- Describe evidence with its unit and scope. “The preserved risk example selects another neighbor when one configured feature changes” is more useful than “The platform delivers intelligent adaptability.”
- Retain uncertainty that reflects the study design. Do not delete “synthetic,” “local,” “planned,” or “not measured” to make prose sound decisive.
- Remove drafting markers and chatbot commentary from submission prose. Keep unresolved facts in notes until supported; never conceal missing evidence through fluency.

Review the argument before polishing sentences: Does the paragraph establish a needed claim? Does its source support the exact wording? Does the conclusion stay within the evidence? Do not optimize for AI detector scores or promise that a passage will evade detection.
